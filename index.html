THE DEFINING PRINCIPLE

“OPUS does not make decisions. It helps people reason with confidence.”
Evidence informs reasoning. Actors remain accountable for decisions.

Organisations do not suffer from a lack of data.

They suffer from a lack of shared understanding.

THE QUESTION

Why do programmes fail?

OPUS began with a practical question drawn from years of technology transformation, programme delivery and assurance.

Programmes rarely fail because of one catastrophic mistake. More often, they drift. Requirements change. Leadership changes. Markets change. The organisation adapts while delivery continues to pursue priorities agreed in a different world.

Capable people make sensible decisions, but often from fragmented evidence, incomplete context and competing interpretations of reality.

The original question therefore evolved.

THE EVOLVING QUESTION

It began with Why do programmes fail?
It became Why do organisations struggle to understand themselves?
It now asks How can people reason with confidence from empirical evidence?

That question became OPUS.

WHAT IS OPUS?

A research programme into organisational knowledge and reasoning.

OPUS investigates how organisations can represent reality, preserve knowledge, reason across evidence and learn from outcomes without transferring decision-making authority away from people.

01

Ontology

Defines the things that exist in an organisation and the relationships that give them meaning.

02

Evidence

Separates observations, assertions and assumptions so that reasoning has an empirical foundation.

03

Reasoning

Connects evidence into explainable conclusions while making uncertainty and assumptions visible.

04

Learning

Records outcomes as new evidence so that understanding can change when reality changes.

THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

OPUS reasons. Actors decide. Outcomes return to the knowledge system as empirical evidence.

Reality is not adjusted to protect the model. The model is adjusted to reflect reality.

EMERGING PRINCIPLES

A theory should be simple enough to remember and strong enough to challenge.

Reality precedes applications. Evidence precedes reasoning. Decisions belong to actors. Confidence is not certainty. Every outcome becomes new evidence. Reality always wins.

POTENTIAL APPLICATIONS

Applications become lenses over shared organisational knowledge.

APEX is not intended to become another isolated enterprise application. It offers a common reasoning layer through which different questions can be explored using the same underlying organisational knowledge.

The value is not in producing another dashboard. It is in explaining how evidence, relationships and context support a conclusion.

IMAGINE ASKING

Opportunity Can we credibly deliver this proposal?
Programme Why are we behind schedule?
Knowledge Who has solved this problem before?

The answer should include the evidence and reasoning—not just the result.

THE RESEARCH

An open and evolving body of work.

Current areas of investigation include organisational ontology, knowledge representation, evidence modelling, explainable reasoning, knowledge graphs, systems thinking, organisational learning and conversational interfaces.

The objective is not to promote a finished answer. It is to test the theory, expose its weaknesses and improve it through practical application.

The technology will evolve. The theory should endure.
